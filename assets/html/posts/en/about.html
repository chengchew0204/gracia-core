A point of centripety

At a southernmost promontory on Mexico's Costa Chica, Original People considered Zipolite to be an underworld of sorts. Since 1970, when transformational travellers pioneered to the then-deserted beach to meet a rare total solar eclipse, Zipolite emerged as a point of centripety for new culture.

Hear a short podcast interview.

Mission

CAMP (in the immediately adjacent Arroyo Tres) aspires to be a welcoming and caring episodic home and cultural location to gather and connect residents with themselves, each other, the local context, and natural surroundings.

Composition

Architecture creates the framework of our lives, it can either open or hinder possibilities, encounters, and connections. The organizing thought, or parti, of CAMP prioritised raw materials and the use of social architecture to support amongness.

Regeneration

Inspired by vernacular structures of coastal Oaxaca, CAMP construction integrates clay, stone, bamboo, palm, and fast-growing sustained-yield guanacaste timber. More than 20 trees were locally reforested for each tree harvested to construct the centre. CAMP is water and energy efficient, with systems and devices to conserve both; and five-stage aerobic digesters that convert wastewater for irrigation and indirect potable water reuse. Twilight lumination reduces CAMP's contribution to light pollution.

Cocreation

Founders Anthony, architect, Maybel, interdisciplinary artist, with Daniel, doctor, are sourcing a body of knowledge to design protopia anchored in wholeness; coleading and learning with culture architects who drive social change and define new models and perspectives in response to this world.

Costa Chica translates to 'small coast' or 'short coast' and refers to this coastal region in southern Mexico. It is known for its wild coastline, surfing, unique Black and Afro-Mexican communities, and natural attractions like whales and sea turtles.
